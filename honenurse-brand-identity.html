Brand identity, version 1.0. A premium clinical learning instrument: concise, calm and exact. Every product is built from this page.

Philosophy

PrincipleWhat it means
ConciseEvery element earns its place. Hierarchy and spacing do the work before decoration.
PremiumComes from typography, proportion, whitespace and consistency. Not from gradients, glows, stacked cards or stock photos.
ClinicalPrecise and calm. Nursing lives in how the content is structured, not in clichés.
The readerStressed, often retaking. The tone is direct and encouraging. A low score is direction, not a verdict.
The pricePremium craft at an affordable price. The design says quality; the price says access.
The rule behind every decision. Do not decorate what structure, typography or hierarchy can communicate. If an element tells the reader nothing, delete it.

Avoid: neon, gradients as a default, heavy rounding, stock healthcare photos, medical clichés (crosses, hearts, stethoscopes, pulse lines) and the generic AI-startup look.

Logo

An H cut with one sharp bevel on each stem, both at the same angle: a single honing stroke. The crossbar is teal. The mark is flat, one shape, and holds at 16 px.

Structure: nursing built into every topic

The identity borrows from nursing's own tools instead of decorating with its symbols. Every topic runs on five beats adapted from the clinical judgment steps the exam now tests, and every number is set like a chart value.

CueWhat the patient shows. AnalysisWhat the cues mean together. PriorityWhat is most at risk first. ActionWhat you do, in order. CheckHow you know it worked.
Digoxin: check the apical pulse first
Apical pulse 52 /min  ·  Hold below 60 /min (adult)  ·  Due 09:00
In plain terms. The apical pulse is the heartbeat counted at the chest, not the wrist.
On the exam. A patient due for digoxin has a pulse of 52 with nausea and new visual changes. The first move is to hold the dose.

Cue. Slow pulse, nausea, visual changes. Analysis. Together they point to digoxin toxicity. Priority. Heart rhythm comes first. Action. Hold the dose, notify the provider, expect a digoxin level and a potassium check. Check. Recount the apical pulse for one full minute.

At the bedside. Tell the patient why you are counting before the medicine.
Patient danger. Slow pulse with dizziness or fainting: stay with the patient and call for help.

Review next: Signs of digoxin toxicity

Layout sample only. Verify every clinical fact before publishing.

Surfaces

SurfaceHow the identity applies
LearnHigh-yield ebooks. On KDP: reflowable EPUB, body in the reader's font, Plex on headings, labels and values. Link text says what the reader gets, such as "Practice this chapter at honenurse.com/pharmacology". Never "free bonus".
PracticeTimed mock exams that look like the real test: one question per screen, timer in Plex Mono, ink on paper. A wrong answer gets an amber label and an icon, never red. Results open with "Review next" and name the topic.
LensThe research extension. A side panel, one job per screen. Setup is two steps: paste your key, press Test.
SiteThree doors of equal weight, in this order: Learn, Practice, Lens. Nothing else on the home page. Footer carries the trademark line below.
